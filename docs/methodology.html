Methods, sources, and boundaries

The project uses one public PBP system across the entire 2006–2025 analysis period and keeps the question deliberately narrow: how strongly does prior performance correlate with next-season performance?

Final metrics

DomainSeason metricOrientation
Individual QBsum(`qb_epa`) / sum(`qb_dropback`) for a stable player ID across teamsHigher is better
Team QBsum(`epa`) / sum(`qb_dropback`) across all team QB dropbacksHigher is better
Offensemean(`epa`) across regular-season pass/rush scrimmage playsHigher is better
Defense−mean(`epa`) allowed on the identical play setHigher is better
Record(wins + 0.5 × ties) / gamesHigher is better

PBP metrics exclude playoffs, special teams, and two-point conversions. EPA/dropback includes attempts, sacks, and scrambles.

Pair construction

For each entity and target season, the prior windows are strict and equally weighted:

1y: metric[t] → metric[t+1]
2y: mean(metric[t−1], metric[t]) → metric[t+1]
5y: mean(metric[t−4] … metric[t]) → metric[t+1]

No window borrows an earlier or later season to fill a gap. For individual QBs, every history year and the target year must meet the playing-time rule.

QB qualification

Headline: at least 14 QB dropbacks per scheduled team game—224 in 2006–2020 and 238 in 2021–2025. This adapts the NFL seasonal passer-qualification convention to the EPA/dropback denominator.

A ≥100-dropback version is retained as a secondary sensitivity, not a replacement headline.

Statistics and checks

  • Headline: Pearson correlation between the prior-window value and next-season value.
  • Rank check: Spearman correlation with average ranks for ties.
  • Uncertainty: 10,000 seeded percentile bootstrap replicates, resampling whole QB or franchise clusters to retain repeated-season dependence.
  • Outliers: rank correlation and the largest leave-one-entity-out change in Pearson r.
  • 2020: a secondary estimate removes any pair whose history or target includes 2020.
  • Schedules: calculated game-by-game and regular season only. Win percentage uses actual completed games: 16 games through 2020, normally 17 afterward, and 16 for Buffalo/Cincinnati in 2022 because their cancelled Week 17 game was not resumed.
  • Franchise continuity: Rams (`STL`/`LA`/`LAR`), Chargers (`SD`/`LAC`), and Raiders (`OAK`/`LV`) are each one entity; Washington stays one franchise.

Sources and provenance

  • nflverse public PBP releases: annual `play_by_play_{season}.csv.gz` files for 2006–2025. nflfastR computes/ships EPA and `qb_epa`; see the QB-EPA documentation.
  • nflverse/nfldata games.csv: completed game scores used to calculate records.
  • Pro Football Reference minimum requirements: reference for the NFL passing-leader qualification convention.
  • Generated provenance manifest: retrieval times, file sizes, SHA-256 checksums, package versions, and validation facts for this build.

Raw third-party PBP and schedule downloads are intentionally excluded from this repository. The build script retrieves them from their sources, caches them locally, and records the checksum. nflverse’s release repository is CC BY 4.0; nflreadr notes that underlying NFL data remain subject to their respective owners’ terms.

Historical DVOA was considered but not used: FTN’s complete archive is subscription-gated and its downloads include regular season plus playoffs, preventing a fully public, consistent regular-season pipeline through 2025.

Material limitations

Correlation is descriptive, not causal. Team/franchise estimates do not adjust for schedule strength, injuries, opponents, or roster turnover. QB EPA/dropback is not an isolated measure of intrinsic QB talent. Most importantly, the strict five-year individual-QB result applies to quarterbacks with five consecutive qualifying seasons plus a qualifying target season, so it is a selected long-tenure population.
